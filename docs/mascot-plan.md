# Faraday, rigged — plan for a Duolingo-grade mascot

Research + implementation plan. Written 2026-09-04.

---

## TL;DR

You are further along than you think, and blocked on something other than code.

Faraday today is **a flipbook of 11 unrelated drawings that hard-cut between
each other**. Duo is **one rigged puppet that blends**. Every difference in
"aliveness" — eyes that follow you, a head that turns, a wave that starts from
whatever pose he was already in — follows from that one structural fact.

The runtime is the easy part. **The bottleneck is that we have no layered art**,
only flat 192px rasters generated by Gemini with the pupils, jaw, hair and arms
all baked into one image. Nothing can be rigged until that is fixed, on either
track. Phase 0 is unavoidable and is most of the real cost.

Recommendation: **Rive** (what Duolingo uses), $9/mo, ~2 weeks after the art
lands. A free no-new-dependency fallback that gets ~70% of the feel is in
§13 if the subscription or the WASM weight is a dealbreaker.

## 2. The gap, in one sentence

**Every Faraday state is a separate drawing, so nothing can interpolate between
them, nothing can look at anything, and every new state costs new art and new
bytes.**

Concretely, what he cannot do today and Duo can:

- **Look at you.** Pupils are painted into the PNG. No eye tracking, ever.
- **Blend.** `happy → thinking` is a cross-dissolve with a scale pop
  (`.faraday-swap`, 300ms). It reads as a slideshow, not a body.
- **Combine.** He cannot wave *while* breathing *while* blinking. Each is a
  separate whole-image animation, and a second `animation` property replaces the
  first — that limitation is documented in the CSS itself.
- **React mid-motion.** A one-shot restarts from frame 0 regardless of where he
  was.
- **Scale.** A new emotion = a Gemini generation + a slice + ~40KB shipped to a
  phone on a filtered school network.

`Faraday25D` is a clever hack around exactly this — it fakes depth from a flat
image because there is no real rig underneath. It is the ceiling of the current
approach, not a step toward the next one.

---

## 3. What Duolingo actually does

Duo runs on **Rive**: a real-time, state-machine-driven animation runtime, not a
pre-rendered timeline. The relevant properties:

- **State machine, not timeline.** The app sets *inputs* (`correct_answer`,
  `streak_milestone`), and the runtime picks the state and blends the transition.
  The app never says "play frames 40-72".
- **Layered blending.** Breathing and blinking run on a base layer underneath
  whatever emotional state is active, so they never stop and never sync.
- **Real-time lip-sync.** Audio analysis maps to Rive inputs driving mouth
  shapes — which is how they localise into dozens of languages without
  re-animating a single frame.
- **One file.** Art + rig + logic ship as a single `.riv`, typically 50-150KB
  for a full character.
- **Split roles.** Animators build timelines; "creative technologists" build the
  state-machine logic and hand engineers a documented input list. That handoff
  contract is the part worth copying even at our scale — see §7.

## 5. Phase 0 — the art (blocking, ~60% of total effort)

Nothing else can start. Current source is `assets-src/faraday-sheet.png`: a flat
raster with everything baked in. `src/assets/faraday-128.svg` is a potrace
autotrace — one `<g>`, 19 monolithic black paths, `fill="#000000"` — useless as
a rig source. There is no vector Faraday.

### What a rig needs

One canonical **front-facing pose at 2048px or higher**, delivered as separate
layers with clean alpha (a layered PSD is ideal — Rive imports PSD directly and
preserves layer names and positions):

```
faraday/
  hair-back            behind everything
  torso
  arm-far-upper        shoulder → elbow, pivot at shoulder
  arm-far-lower        elbow → wrist, pivot at elbow
  hand-far
  head                 skull + face, NO eyes, NO brows, NO mouth
  eye-white-a          the sclera, both drawn separately
  eye-white-b
  pupil-a              free-floating, sits over the sclera
  pupil-b
  eyelid-a             closed-eye shape, hidden at rest
  eyelid-b
  brow-a               free-floating over the forehead
  brow-b
  mouth-closed         the neutral mouth
  mouth-open           an open "ah" shape, same anchor
  hair-side-a          the two hair masses; a/b are positions in the ARTWORK
  hair-side-b            — never start/end, the drawing does not mirror (below)
  collar-bowtie
  arm-near-upper
  arm-near-lower
  hand-near
```

**Naming rule, non-negotiable.** `a`/`b`, never `left`/`right`, never
`start`/`end`. The drawing does not mirror under RTL and never will —
`Faraday25D` already learned this and documents it in `src/index.css` around
`.faraday-25d-wing-a`. A "start-side arm" is a bug waiting for a Hebrew layout.

**Everything under a moving part must be painted in.** The skin behind the brows,
the sclera behind the pupils, the neck behind the jaw. If it is not painted, you
get a hole the moment it moves 3px.

### Three ways to get it, cheapest first

1. ~~**Cut it yourself from the existing sheet (~6-10h, $0).**~~ **DONE** —
   `scripts/cut-rig-layers.mjs` → `assets-src/faraday-rig/`, 12 layers on one
   1024px canvas. Cut in code rather than by hand: the art is flat fills inside
   thick outlines, so flood-filling from a seed per region does the same job as
   a lasso tool, and the occluded regions inpaint *exactly* (they are closed
   shapes, so they are filled with their own colour rather than a guess).
   Verified by moving the parts — pupils slide over a full sclera, brows lift
   off clean skin, no holes. Two ceilings, both expected and both needing (3):
   the hair is one mass, and the head has a bite out of it under each hair wing
   (a seam opens past ~15px of relative movement).

2. **Regenerate on-model, layer by layer, with Gemini (~4h + API cost).**
   The pipeline and prompt discipline already exist in `assets-src/README.md`:
   flat magenta backdrop (his palette has no magenta), no motion blur, nothing in
   the outer 10%, hold poses 2s. The `nano-banana` skill covers this.
   **Risk: consistency.** Generating parts independently drifts off-model — the
   README already records framing instructions being ignored twice. Mitigate by
   seeding every part from the same canonical frame, the way
   `make-turntable-seed.mjs` already does. Expect several rounds.

3. **Commission an illustrator to redraw him as layered vector (~$250-700, 1-2wk).**
   This is the honest answer to "real quality". Someone who has rigged a
   character before will deliver a layered file that rigs cleanly the first time,
   plus a turnaround and a style guide you can generate against forever. Brief:
   the layer list above, plus "must rig in Rive with bones and meshes", plus the
   existing PNGs as the model reference.

**Recommendation:** do (1) now to de-risk the rig and prove the track this week,
then (3) once the rig works and you know exactly what you are paying for.

### Deliverable

`assets-src/faraday-rig.psd` (or a folder of named PNGs) plus a one-page model
sheet. Add both to `assets-src/README.md` with the same rebuild-instructions
discipline the other entries already have.

## 6. Phase 1 — the rig (Rive, ~2-3 days)

Import the PSD. Rive keeps layer names and positions. Then:

### Bones

```
root
└── spine            slight lean, breathing
    └── chest
        ├── neck
        │   └── head          rotation + translation, capped ±18°
        │       └── jaw       one axis, drives mouth open
        ├── arm-a-upper       IK target at the hand
        │   └── arm-a-lower
        └── arm-b-upper
            └── arm-b-lower
```

### Meshes

Add a mesh (contour + interior vertices) and bind to bones for anything that
should bend rather than pivot rigidly:

- **hair-side-a/b** — the highest-value mesh. Secondary motion on the hair is
  most of what sells "alive". Weight the tips loose, the roots tight.
- **collar-bowtie** — small follow-through on head turn.
- **torso** — gentle squash for breathing.

Arms can stay rigid segments; a cartoon elbow reads fine as a pivot.

### Constraints

- **Pupils**: translation constraint, clamped to the sclera bounds, driven by
  `lookX`/`lookY`. Clamp *hard* — a pupil escaping the eye is instant uncanny.
- **Head**: rotation driven at ~35% of `lookX`, so the head leads the eyes
  slightly. That 35% is a taste knob; tune it against a real phone.
- **Brows**: translation + rotation, driven by `mood`.
- **Arms**: IK, so a wave is one target moving rather than two rotations
  hand-keyed.

### Timelines to author

| Timeline | Length | Loop | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `breathe` | 4.2s | yes | port the curve from `@keyframes faraday-breathe` — already tuned |
| `blink` | 180ms | one-shot | fired on a random interval, never on a fixed beat |
| `idle` | — | — | pose only; `breathe` runs additively over it |
| `thinking` | 3.6s | yes | port `@keyframes faraday-ponder`, keep it off-tempo from breathe |
| `happy` | 1.2s | one-shot → hold | |
| `wrong` | 1.0s | one-shot → hold | open palm, encouraging, not disappointed |
| `streak` | 1.4s | yes | amber, higher amplitude |
| `celebrate` | 2.0s | one-shot | replaces the 12-frame sprite |
| `talking` | 0.83s | yes | replaces the typing sprite; jaw-driven |
| `wave` | 1.6s | one-shot | |
| `point` | 0.8s | one-shot → hold | |
| `bored` | 3s | one-shot | idle break — see §10 |

Port the existing curves rather than re-inventing them. `faraday-breathe` at
4.2s against `faraday-ponder` at 3.6s is a deliberately non-harmonic pair; that
detail is already right and is easy to lose in a rebuild.

## 7. Phase 2 — the state machine (~2 days)

**This is the API the app codes against. Freeze it early and write it down** —
this is the animator→engineer handoff contract Duolingo splits a whole role over.

### Inputs

| Input | Type | Range | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `mood` | number | 0-7 | 0 idle, 1 thinking, 2 happy, 3 wrong, 4 streak, 5 celebrate, 6 talking, 7 wave |
| `lookX` | number | -1..1 | b-side ← → a-side, in artwork space |
| `lookY` | number | -1..1 | down → up |
| `energy` | number | 0..1 | global amplitude; maps to streak length |
| `react` | trigger | — | one-shot accent over the current mood |
| `blink` | trigger | — | fired from JS on a random interval |

Six inputs. Resist adding a seventh — every input is a thing 15 call sites can
get wrong.

### Layers

1. **base** — the `mood` state graph. Every transition gets a real blend time:
   180ms for neighbours (idle↔thinking), 260ms for opposites (wrong→celebrate).
   This blend is the single thing that separates it from today's cross-dissolve.
2. **breath** — `breathe`, always on, additive, amplitude scaled by `energy`.
3. **blink** — one-shot on `blink`, additive.
4. **look** — `lookX`/`lookY` driving the pupil and head constraints, additive,
   independent of everything else.

Layered and additive is the whole point: he can be *thinking*, *breathing*,
*blinking*, and *looking at the answer field* at the same time. Today he can do
exactly one of those.

### Deliverable

`src/assets/faraday.riv` plus a table of these inputs in `assets-src/README.md`.
Target under 200KB; if it comes in over 300KB, downscale the source images
inside Rive before touching the rig.

## 8. Phase 3 — integration (~2 days)

```bash
npm i @rive-app/react-canvas
```

Use `@rive-app/canvas-lite` if the rig ends up using no Rive Text, Layouts,
Scripting or Audio — it is meaningfully smaller. Decide after Phase 2.

### The four things that will actually bite

**1. Self-host the WASM. Non-negotiable.**
Rive loads its WASM from `unpkg.com` by default. `CLAUDE.md` rule 6: *never load
assets from a CDN — school networks filter them.* A filtered unpkg means a
mascot that silently never appears for exactly the students who matter.

```ts
// src/lib/rive.ts
import { RuntimeLoader } from "@rive-app/react-canvas";
import wasmUrl from "@rive-app/react-canvas/rive.wasm?url"; // Vite fingerprints + self-hosts

RuntimeLoader.setWasmUrl(wasmUrl);
```

The WASM version must match the npm package version — they are built together,
so a `?url` import off the package (not a copied file) is what keeps them in
step through upgrades. Verify in the network tab that nothing hits `unpkg`.

**2. Keep the existing prop shape.** Fifteen call sites pass
`pose`/`px`/`fill`/`glow`/`className`. The new component takes the same props and
maps `pose → mood` internally. One file changes, not fifteen.

**3. Lazy-load it; PNG is the fallback.** 200KB should not land on
`TeacherDashboard` (already 531KB) or on first paint.

```tsx
const FaradayRive = lazy(() => import("./FaradayRive"));

<Suspense fallback={<FaradayAvatar pose={pose} px={px} />}>
  <FaradayRive pose={pose} px={px} />
</Suspense>
```

The PNG mascot stays in the tree permanently as the fallback and the
reduced-motion path. Do not delete it. It is also the answer if the `.riv` fails
to load — same `onError` discipline `FaradayAvatar` already has.

**4. `prefers-reduced-motion`.** Do not just slow it down — pause the state
machine and hold a pose, exactly as the CSS already does for `.faraday-typing`
("the global reduced-motion rule only crushes duration, which would spin a
12-frame loop into a flicker"). Same trap, same fix.

### Also

- **RTL:** never mirror him. He is a person, not a chevron. `lookX` is in
  artwork space; if a UI element's side needs to flip, flip the *sign you pass*,
  at the call site, not the character.
- **Design tokens:** the `.riv` bakes its own colours, which
  `scripts/design-lint.mjs` cannot see. Add the mascot's palette to `DESIGN.md`
  by hand so it does not silently drift from the clay/electric-green system.
- **Kill the `<canvas>` when offscreen** — `IntersectionObserver` → pause. Rive's
  idle CPU is near zero when no input changes, but a paused canvas is zero.

## 9. Phase 4 — wire the app (~1 day)

Map real events to inputs. Everything below already exists as a trigger point:

| Where | Event | Input |
|---|---|---|
| `PracticeSession` | answer correct | `mood=2`, `react` |
| `PracticeSession` | answer wrong | `mood=3`, `react` |
| `PracticeSession` | streak ≥3 | `mood=4`, `energy = min(streak/10, 1)` |
| `PracticeSession` | question loaded, idle >4s | `mood=1` (thinking) |
| `PracticeSession` | focus on answer field | `lookX/lookY` toward the field |
| `AIChatPanel` | assistant streaming | `mood=6` (talking) |
| `StudentHome` | first visit of the day | `mood=7` (wave) |
| `FaradayTour` | step change | `mood=0`, `lookX/lookY` toward the highlighted element |
| `SessionRecap` | session complete | `mood=5` (celebrate) |
| `Onboarding` | greeting | `mood=7` |

**Pointer look-at** (desktop only — reuse the `pointerType === "touch"` guard
`Faraday25D` already has):

```ts
const look = (e: PointerEvent) => {
  const b = el.getBoundingClientRect();
  rive.setNumber("lookX", clamp((e.clientX - (b.left + b.width / 2)) / 400, -1, 1));
  rive.setNumber("lookY", clamp(((b.top + b.height / 2) - e.clientY) / 400, -1, 1));
};
```

On touch, point him at the focused input instead — same two numbers, different
source. On a phone that is where the student is looking anyway.

## 10. Phase 5 — the things that make it Duo

Ordered by feel-per-hour. Ship 1-3; the rest are optional.

1. **Eye tracking.** Single highest-value item in this entire document. The
   moment his eyes follow the cursor he stops being a sticker.
2. **He reacts before the toast.** ~120ms of anticipation before the answer
   feedback appears reads as *him* judging the answer rather than the UI telling
   him about it.
3. **Idle break.** After ~20s of no interaction, play `bored` — checks a pocket
   watch, taps a foot, sags. This is most of what makes Duo feel present rather
   than paused. Randomise the interval so it is never predictable.
4. **`energy` from the streak.** Same animations, higher amplitude, at streak 8
   vs streak 1. Free variety from a number you already track.
5. **Look at the thing that just changed.** He glances at a level-up badge, a new
   homework card. Two numbers, big payoff.
6. **Lip-sync.** Only worth it if you add TTS. `AnalyserNode` RMS → `jaw`. The
   Web Audio API does this in ~15 lines with no dependency; it is the same
   technique Duolingo uses and it is genuinely easy once the jaw bone exists.

Rive's free tier is **editor-only — it cannot export**. You can build and
evaluate the entire rig for $0 and only pay when you ship. There is no runtime
or per-user fee; exports keep working if you later cancel.

Context on the weight: the app currently ships ~2.8MB of JS
(`MathField` 819KB, `index` 740KB, `TeacherDashboard` 531KB). 200KB lazy-loaded
on student routes only is real but not the biggest thing on the page. Measure it
on a throttled phone before and after — do not take that on faith.

## 12. Risks

- **Art consistency (high).** The single most likely failure. `assets-src/README.md`
  already documents Gemini ignoring framing instructions twice. Budget for
  several rounds, or commission it.
- **200KB WASM on school networks (medium).** Lazy-loaded and cached, but test on
  the actual pilot network before rollout. The PNG fallback must stay wired.
- **Rive subscription lapses (low).** Exported `.riv` files keep working; only
  re-exporting needs the seat. Commit the `.riv` **and** the `.rev` source to the
  repo.
- **Uncanny valley (medium).** Rigged characters look *worse* than static ones
  until the rig is good — blend times too long, pupils escaping the sclera, hair
  mesh popping. Keep `/mascot` as the review surface and check every state on a
  real phone at real sizes.
- **A fourth animation library (low, but real).** framer-motion + gsap + animejs
  + Rive. Rive should *replace* the mascot's use of the other three, not stack on
  top. Delete `.faraday-sprite`, `.faraday-typing`, `.faraday-swap`,
  `.faraday-ponder`, `.faraday-breathe` and probably all of `Faraday25D` when the
  rig lands — about 200 lines of CSS and 126 of TSX. Grep every caller first;
  this project has had three "safe" cuts turn out load-bearing.

## 13. Track A in full — the free fallback

If $9/mo or 200KB is a no: this gets ~70% of the feel with zero new dependencies.

Same Phase 0. Then export the layers as **one inline SVG** with a `<g>` per part
and an explicit `transform-origin` on each — that is the rig. GSAP (already in
`src/lib/gsapUtils.ts`) drives it.

```tsx
// Eye tracking: two transforms, no library work at all.
gsap.to([pupilA, pupilB], { x: lookX * 4, y: lookY * 3, duration: 0.4, ease: "power2.out" });
gsap.to(head,             { rotation: lookX * 6, duration: 0.6, ease: "power2.out" });
```

A GSAP timeline per mood; switching moods is `tl.tweenTo()` from wherever the
parts currently are — which *is* a blend, just a hand-rolled one. `breathe` and
`blink` are separate infinite timelines on separate elements, so they layer for
free.

**What you get:** eye tracking, head turn, real blended transitions, layered
breathing and blinking, arm gestures, idle breaks, unlimited new states at zero
KB.
**What you don't:** mesh deformation (no hair follow-through, no cloth), and
every timing number is hand-tuned in code instead of scrubbed in a GUI.
Iteration is maybe 4× slower, which compounds over a year.

~3-4 days after the art. Honestly good. It is not Duo, and the gap is mostly the
hair.

## 14. Order of work

1. ~~**Cut layers from `faraday-sheet.png`.**~~ **DONE** —
   `node scripts/cut-rig-layers.mjs` → `assets-src/faraday-rig/` (12 layers,
   one 1024px canvas, `manifest.json` with pivots). See §5.
2. **Rig it in Rive on the free tier.** Import the folder — the layers are all
   on the same canvas, so they land in register. Bones, eye constraint,
   `breathe`, `blink`, three moods. Do not export. (1-2 days)
3. **Look at it.** Eye tracking plus a blended `idle↔thinking` is the whole bet.
   If that does not feel obviously better than what ships today, stop — take
   Track A, or stop entirely.
4. **If it does:** commission the real art (§5.3) against the rig you now
   understand. (1-2 weeks, parallel)
5. Subscribe, export, integrate (§8), wire (§9). (5 days)
6. Ship behind a flag on `green-torch`. Both mascots live side by side at
   `/mascot`. Compare on a real phone.
7. Delete the old CSS animation stack and `Faraday25D` — grep every caller first.

Step 3 is a real gate. Two days to find out whether this is worth two weeks.
